Can Hypothyroidism Cause Joint Pain and Muscle Aches?

November 20, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Yes. Joint and muscle pain is on the early symptom list for hypothyroidism at MedlinePlus and the NIDDK. In a 2000 prospective study, 79% of people with newly found hypothyroidism had muscle or nerve complaints. Treatment usually helps, but weakness can be slow to clear.

How common are aches and pains with an underactive thyroid?

They are common. MedlinePlus lists "joint or muscle pain" and "weakness" among the early symptoms of hypothyroidism, next to fatigue, feeling cold, and constipation. The NIDDK lists "joint and muscle pain" on its Hashimoto's disease page too.

The best numbers come from a small Dutch study. Duyff and colleagues (2000) followed adults with newly diagnosed hypothyroidism. They found:

  • 79% had neuromuscular complaints, such as pain, cramps, or weakness.
  • 38% had measurable weakness in one or more muscle groups.
  • 42% had signs of a mild nerve problem (sensorimotor axonal neuropathy).
  • 29% had carpal tunnel syndrome.

These were people with new, untreated disease. People whose levels are well controlled on medicine should not expect rates this high. For other early signs, see our guide to hypothyroidism warning signs.

What do thyroid-related muscle problems feel like?

A 1995 review in Seminars in Arthritis and Rheumatism describes a range, "from myalgias and arthralgias to true myopathy and arthritis." In plain terms, most people get the milder end:

  • Aches (myalgia). A dull, general soreness in the muscles.
  • Cramps and stiffness. A 2016 review lists generalized muscle stiffness among the musculoskeletal effects of primary hypothyroidism.
  • Weakness. The Duyff study measured it with strength testing, so it is more than just feeling tired.
  • Slow reflexes. MedlinePlus notes reflexes with "a delayed relaxation phase" on exam.

Raised CK. Creatine kinase (CK) is an enzyme that leaks from muscle. A 2009 study of 25 people with marked hypothyroidism (TSH above 30) found high CK in 14 of them. The highest value was 1,221 U/L. The Duyff study found that CK level did not match how weak people were. A high CK can therefore come from the thyroid alone, but it still needs an explanation.

Hypothyroid myopathy and Hoffmann syndrome. True muscle disease (myopathy) is less common. Hoffmann syndrome is a rare form with stiffness, cramps, and muscles that look bigger than normal (pseudohypertrophy). A 2024 case report describes a 20-year-old who stopped her thyroid medicine after pregnancy. She developed painful, swollen calf muscles with cramps.

What happens in the joints?

Joint pain (arthralgia) is the most common joint symptom. The 1995 Yale review describes a specific hypothyroid arthropathy: stiff joints with thick, non-inflamed fluid. It mostly affects the knees, wrists, and hands. A 1975 Lancet paper notes that fluid collections from hypothyroidism are rare but can appear before the typical signs, so they can mislead doctors.

The 2016 review also links hypothyroidism to frozen shoulder, trigger finger, Dupuytren's contracture, and crystal arthritis (gout and pseudogout). The Yale review adds a caution: nerve and muscle symptoms can feel like a joint problem when the joint itself is fine.

Hand numbness and tingling from carpal tunnel syndrome is a separate issue caused by swelling at the wrist. We cover it in can hypothyroidism cause swelling.

Does levothyroxine fix the aches, and how fast?

For most people, aches improve as thyroid levels return to normal. The treatment is levothyroxine. Both the ATA and the NIDDK say your doctor will check your blood about 6 to 8 weeks after you start or change a dose.

No guideline we found gives a fixed timeline for aches to go away. The Duyff study gives the best clue. After 1 year of treatment, 13% of the hypothyroid group still had weakness. The authors wrote that "weakness in hypothyroidism is more difficult to treat" than weakness from an overactive thyroid.

A fair way to think about it:

  1. First 6 to 8 weeks: dose is adjusted. Do not expect full relief yet.
  2. Once TSH is in range: aches and cramps often ease over the following months.
  3. After several months at a normal TSH: if pain or weakness is unchanged, look for another cause.

If you feel unwell with normal blood tests, read still have symptoms with a normal TSH.

Could Hashimoto's be linked to another autoimmune joint disease?

Yes, and this is the step people miss. Hashimoto's is an autoimmune disease, and autoimmune diseases tend to cluster. The NIDDK says you are more likely to have hypothyroidism if you have rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, Sjögren's syndrome, celiac disease, or type 1 diabetes.

A 2012 review in the Journal of Rheumatology notes that arthritis now shows up in people with autoimmune thyroid disease even when their thyroid levels are normal. A 2015 follow-up review links autoimmune thyroiditis to fibromyalgia and other chronic widespread pain.

So if joints are swollen, hot, or stiff for a long time each morning, levothyroxine alone may not be the answer. Ask whether a rheumatology workup makes sense.

Why do statins matter if you have hypothyroidism?

Hypothyroidism raises cholesterol, so many people take a statin as well. The FDA label for atorvastatin (Lipitor) lists "uncontrolled hypothyroidism" as a risk factor for myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. The other listed factors are age 65 or older, kidney problems, some other drugs, and higher doses.

The label tells patients to report "unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness," especially with fever or tiredness. If you have new aches on a statin, ask for a TSH check as well as a CK. Our post on hypothyroidism and high cholesterol covers the statin question in more detail.

When do aches mean something else, and what should you ask?

Muscle pain has many causes. MedlinePlus lists overuse, fibromyalgia, flu and other infections, lupus, polymyalgia rheumatica, polymyositis, low potassium or calcium, and certain medicines. Those medicines include statins and ACE inhibitors for blood pressure. If you take one, our sister site hypertension.md covers blood pressure drugs.

MedlinePlus says to contact a provider if muscle pain lasts more than 3 days, is severe, follows a new medicine or dose, or comes with weakness, high fever, or a stiff neck.

A checklist for your visit:

  • TSH and free T4, with the date of your last result and your current dose.
  • CK, if you have weakness, cramps, or take a statin.
  • Thyroid antibodies (TPO), if Hashimoto's has not been confirmed.
  • Potassium and calcium, which MedlinePlus lists as causes of muscle pain.
  • A note of which joints hurt, whether they swell, and how long morning stiffness lasts.
  • A full medicine list, including statins, blood pressure drugs, and supplements.
  • A direct question: "Should I see a rheumatologist?" if joints are swollen or pain spreads.

The bottom line

Hypothyroidism often causes muscle aches, cramps, stiffness, and joint pain, and it can raise CK. Most people improve once levothyroxine brings TSH into range, though weakness may take many months. If aches stay after your levels are normal, check for statin effects, other autoimmune disease, and other common causes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an underactive thyroid cause leg cramps at night?

Muscle cramps and stiffness are listed among the musculoskeletal effects of hypothyroidism. Cramps also have many other causes, including low potassium or calcium and some medicines. If cramps are new and you have other thyroid symptoms, ask for a TSH test.

Is a high CK level dangerous if it comes from my thyroid?

In one study, 14 of 25 people with marked hypothyroidism had a raised CK. CK alone did not match how weak people were. A high CK still needs an explanation, especially if you take a statin or have new weakness.

Can subclinical hypothyroidism cause muscle pain?

It can in some people. A 1992 study found clear muscle effects mainly once TSH rose above 12. A 2024 case report describes Hoffmann syndrome in a woman with subclinical disease who had stopped her medicine. Its authors call this rare in subclinical disease, so look for other causes too.

Should I see a rheumatologist or an endocrinologist for joint pain with Hashimoto's?

Start with whoever manages your thyroid and confirm your TSH is in range. If joints are swollen, warm, or stiff for a long time each morning, ask for a rheumatology referral. Hashimoto's is linked to rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, and Sjogren's syndrome.

Will exercise make thyroid muscle aches worse?

MedlinePlus says gentle stretching and regular exercise help many muscle aches. Untreated hypothyroidism can cause real weakness, so build up slowly. Stop and get checked if exercise causes severe pain or weakness that does not settle.

Can Hypothyroidism Cause Insomnia or Poor Sleep?

November 23, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Hypothyroidism more often causes tiredness than true insomnia. Trouble sleeping is a classic sign of the opposite problem, an overactive thyroid, or of a levothyroxine dose that is too high. The link also runs both ways: a 2021 study in Endocrine found that poor sleep was tied to a raised TSH.

Does an underactive thyroid cause insomnia?

Not in the classic way. The main symptom lists for hypothyroidism name fatigue, not sleeplessness. The NIDDK lists fatigue, weight gain, trouble tolerating cold, and joint and muscle pain. The American Thyroid Association says that when thyroid levels are low, "your body slows down," and you may feel tired. MedlinePlus lists fatigue or "feeling slowed down" and weakness as early signs.

So the usual pattern is the reverse of insomnia. People with low thyroid hormone tend to feel sleepy, sluggish, and unrefreshed. They may sleep long hours and still wake tired. That said, a person can have hypothyroidism and insomnia at the same time. Insomnia is common, and the NHLBI lists many causes that have nothing to do with the thyroid, such as stress, shift work, caffeine, and screens close to bedtime.

Is it insomnia or daytime fatigue?

The two feel alike after a bad week, but they are different problems. Insomnia means trouble falling asleep or staying asleep. Fatigue means low energy during the day, even after enough hours in bed. Hypothyroidism points toward the second.

A short self-check can help you describe the problem to your doctor:

  • Insomnia pattern: you lie awake, wake often, or wake too early and cannot fall back asleep.
  • Fatigue pattern: you fall asleep fine and sleep long, but you still feel drained and slow all day.
  • Breathing pattern: you snore loudly, gasp at night, or a partner notices pauses in breathing. This can point to sleep apnea, which has its own thyroid link, covered in our post on whether hypothyroidism can cause sleep apnea.

How do hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism differ for sleep?

Too little thyroid hormone slows the body. Too much speeds it up, and that is where trouble sleeping shows up most clearly.

  • Hypothyroidism (low): fatigue, feeling cold, feeling slowed down, low mood (NIDDK, MedlinePlus).
  • Hyperthyroidism (high): the NIDDK lists nervousness, irritability, a rapid heartbeat, and "trouble sleeping." The ATA lists "difficulty sleeping," and MedlinePlus lists restlessness and "sleep problems."

One overlap can confuse people. Hyperthyroidism can also cause fatigue and muscle weakness, per the NIDDK. A person can feel wired at night and worn out during the day. Racing thoughts at night can also look like anxiety, so a thyroid test is a sensible early step.

Can too much levothyroxine cause insomnia?

Yes. This is the clearest thyroid cause of insomnia for people already on treatment. The FDA label for Synthroid (levothyroxine) says its adverse reactions are "primarily those of hyperthyroidism due to therapeutic overdosage." Insomnia is on that list, along with nervousness, irritability, palpitations, tremors, and heat intolerance.

The ATA gives a plain version: too much medicine can make you feel nervous, have a fast heartbeat, feel hot, or lose weight. Too little can leave you tired, cold, or constipated.

A study of people with thyroid cancer adds some detail. Many of these patients take levothyroxine at doses that push TSH down on purpose. A 2021 study in Hormone and Metabolic Research compared 191 patients with 79 healthy controls. Sleep quality scores were worse in patients than in controls. The suppressed-TSH group scored a mean of 7.94 on the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, against 5.35 for controls (higher means worse sleep). Lower TSH tracked weakly with worse sleep scores. If new insomnia started after a dose increase, read our guide to signs your levothyroxine dose is too high.

Can poor sleep raise your TSH?

It may. TSH follows a daily rhythm, so sleep can affect the number on a lab report. A 2021 study in Endocrine compared 168 people with an isolated high TSH against 119 people with normal TSH. Poor sleep was more common in the high-TSH group (70.24% vs 49.58%). After adjusting for other factors, poor sleep carried about 2.4 times the odds of a raised TSH.

The follow-up is the striking part. Among people whose sleep improved, TSH returned to normal in 85.42%. Among those who still slept poorly, only 6.45% normalized. The authors advise doctors to check sleep and repeat the TSH test once sleep improves. This was one study at one center, so it does not prove cause. But a mildly high TSH after weeks of bad sleep may be worth rechecking before treatment changes.

Does taking levothyroxine at bedtime affect sleep?

No trial so far has shown that bedtime dosing helps or harms sleep itself. The trials measured thyroid levels and general quality of life, not sleep.

  • A 2010 double-blind crossover trial in the Netherlands enrolled 105 patients, and 90 finished. Bedtime dosing lowered TSH by 1.25 mIU/L compared with morning dosing. Quality of life and fatigue scores did not change.
  • A 2022 crossover trial in Brazil enrolled 201 adults aged 60 or older. Mean TSH was 2.95 with morning dosing and 3.64 with bedtime dosing, a difference that was not statistically significant.

The FDA label says to take levothyroxine once a day, preferably on an empty stomach, one-half to one hour before breakfast. MedlinePlus notes you can ask your provider whether bedtime dosing suits you. Do not switch on your own: a change in timing can shift your TSH, so your doctor may want a recheck. Our post on taking levothyroxine on an empty stomach covers food and timing rules.

What should you check if you have thyroid disease and cannot sleep?

This checklist pulls together the points above. Bring it to your next visit.

  1. Get a current TSH. A low TSH on levothyroxine suggests the dose may be too high, which the label ties to insomnia.
  2. Note when the insomnia started. A start within weeks of a dose increase or a brand switch is worth mentioning.
  3. Look for other overactive signs. A racing heart, tremor, sweating, heat intolerance, or weight loss point toward too much hormone (NIDDK).
  4. Separate insomnia from fatigue. If you sleep long but wake exhausted, the issue may be undertreatment or another cause, not insomnia.
  5. Ask about snoring and breathing pauses. These need a sleep apnea check, not a dose change.
  6. Review common sleep disruptors. Caffeine, alcohol, late screens, stress, and shift work all raise insomnia risk (NHLBI).
  7. Recheck a mildly high TSH after sleep improves, as the 2021 Endocrine study suggests.

If insomnia persists once thyroid levels are normal, treat it as its own problem. insomnia.md covers causes and treatments for chronic insomnia.

The bottom line

Hypothyroidism usually makes people tired, not sleepless. True insomnia points more toward an overactive thyroid or a levothyroxine dose that is too high, which the FDA label lists among its overdose effects. Poor sleep may also raise TSH by itself. Bedtime dosing of levothyroxine has not been shown to help or hurt sleep. If you cannot sleep, a TSH test and a talk about timing and dose are a sensible first step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why am I so tired with hypothyroidism but still sleeping a lot?

Fatigue is one of the most common symptoms of an underactive thyroid, according to the NIDDK. Low thyroid hormone slows the body down, so long sleep may still feel unrefreshing. If tiredness continues once your TSH is in range, ask your doctor to look for other causes.

Is insomnia a side effect of levothyroxine?

The FDA label lists insomnia among adverse reactions, and says these reactions mostly come from too high a dose. At the right dose, levothyroxine replaces a hormone your body already makes. New insomnia after a dose increase is worth a TSH check.

Should I take levothyroxine at night to sleep better?

A 2010 trial of bedtime dosing measured thyroid levels and quality of life, not sleep, and found no change in quality of life or fatigue. The FDA label says to take it preferably on an empty stomach, half an hour to an hour before breakfast. Ask your doctor before changing the time you take it.

Can lack of sleep make my thyroid test look abnormal?

A 2021 study found that poor sleep was linked to a raised TSH. Among people whose sleep improved, most saw TSH return to normal. If your TSH is only slightly high after a stretch of poor sleep, a repeat test may be reasonable.

Does an overactive thyroid cause trouble sleeping?

Yes. The NIDDK, the American Thyroid Association, and MedlinePlus all list trouble sleeping as a symptom of hyperthyroidism. It often comes with nervousness, a fast heartbeat, and heat intolerance.

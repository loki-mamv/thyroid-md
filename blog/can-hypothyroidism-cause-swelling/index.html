Can Hypothyroidism Cause Swelling in the Face, Hands, or Legs?

The short answer: Yes. Long-standing hypothyroidism can make the face, eyelids, hands, and feet look puffy. A 2011 review in Dermato-Endocrinology calls this generalized myxedema the classic skin sign of an underactive thyroid. It comes from sugar-protein molecules building up in the skin, so it often does not dent when pressed.

Why does an underactive thyroid make you puffy?

The old medical name for hypothyroidism is myxedema. MedlinePlus still lists "myxedema" as an alternative name for the condition. The word describes the skin change, not a separate disease.

In the skin, low thyroid hormone lets molecules called glycosaminoglycans build up in the deeper layer (the dermis). The main one is hyaluronic acid, according to the Safer review. These molecules hold water. The tissue looks swollen, but the review notes the skin "does not pit with pressure."

This is different from the usual fluid swelling (edema) that collects in the ankles from heart or vein problems. That kind of swelling often leaves a dent when you press it with a finger. The difference is a useful clue, but it is not a test. Some people have both kinds at once.

Where on the body does thyroid swelling show up?

MedlinePlus lists "puffy face, hands, and feet" among the late symptoms of untreated hypothyroidism. Its exam findings include "swelling of the arms and legs" and coarse facial features. The common places are:

  • Face and eyelids. Puffiness around the eyes and a fuller, coarser look to the face.
  • Hands and fingers. Rings may feel tight. The skin may feel thick and dry.
  • Feet and lower legs. Firm, doughy swelling that does not hold a dent well.

Note the word "late." Swelling usually appears after months of untreated or undertreated disease. The American Thyroid Association lists early symptoms as feeling cold, tired, constipated, and having dry skin. For the full list, see our guide to hypothyroidism warning signs.

Hands and carpal tunnel syndrome. Hypothyroidism is linked to carpal tunnel syndrome, but the link is weaker than many websites suggest. MedlinePlus explains that any swelling inside the narrow carpal tunnel at the wrist can pinch the median nerve. That causes numbness, tingling, or weakness in the thumb and first fingers.

A 2014 meta-analysis in Muscle and Nerve pooled 18 studies. Studies that did not adjust for other factors showed about twice the risk of carpal tunnel syndrome with hypothyroidism. When the analysis used only studies that adjusted for confounders, the risk fell to about 1.4 times. The author called it "only a modest association." If you have new hand numbness and other hypothyroid symptoms, a TSH test is a fair question to ask. Do not assume the thyroid is the only cause.

How long does the swelling take to go away on treatment?

Treatment is thyroid hormone replacement, usually levothyroxine. Both the NIDDK and the ATA say your doctor will check your blood about 6 to 8 weeks after you start or change a dose. Your dose is adjusted until TSH is in the target range.

We found no reliable source that gives a set timeline for puffiness to clear. Myxedema develops slowly, and the deposits clear gradually once hormone levels are normal. In practice, expect the face and hands to improve over weeks to months, not days. If puffiness stays after your blood tests are normal for several months, ask your doctor to look for another cause. Our levothyroxine guide covers how dosing and follow-up work.

How can you tell thyroid swelling from other causes?

Swelling in the legs has many causes, and most are not the thyroid. MedlinePlus lists heart failure, kidney failure, liver failure, blood clots, vein problems, infection, pregnancy, and long periods of sitting or standing. Some medicines also cause it:

  • Blood pressure medicines called calcium channel blockers
  • Hormones, including estrogen and testosterone
  • Some antidepressants, including MAO inhibitors and tricyclics
  • Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)

If a blood pressure medicine is the cause, the fix is often a change of drug. Our sister site hypertension.md covers blood pressure treatment and its side effects.

Clues that point away from the thyroid: swelling that dents deeply, swelling only in one leg, or swelling with shortness of breath. MedlinePlus notes that heart failure causes leg and ankle swelling, weight gain over a few days, and waking up short of breath. Clues that point toward the thyroid: firm puffiness of the face and hands, plus cold intolerance, fatigue, dry skin, and constipation.

Before a visit, write down what you notice. MedlinePlus says doctors will ask similar questions. Note:

  1. Which body parts swell: face, eyelids, hands, ankles, or legs above the knee.
  2. Whether one side or both sides swell.
  3. Whether a finger press leaves a dent that lasts.
  4. Whether it is worse in the morning or the evening, and whether raising your legs helps.
  5. When it started, and any new medicine or dose change around that time.
  6. Other symptoms: tiredness, feeling cold, weight change, shortness of breath, or hand numbness.
  7. Your last TSH result and date, and your current levothyroxine dose if you take it.

Is pretibial myxedema the same thing?

No. Pretibial myxedema (also called thyroid dermopathy) usually happens with Graves disease, the most common cause of an overactive thyroid. The ATA describes it as a rare "lumpy reddish thickening of the skin in front of the shins." It is usually painless and mild.

A 2005 review from the Mayo Clinic explains that it is an autoimmune problem. Antibodies against the TSH receptor stimulate skin cells to make large amounts of glycosaminoglycans. It occasionally occurs in Hashimoto thyroiditis. Almost all cases come with fairly severe thyroid eye disease, which usually appears first. Most cases need no treatment, and about half of mild cases clear on their own after several years. If you had Graves disease, see our post on thyroid eye disease after treatment.

When is swelling an emergency?

Myxedema coma (also called myxedema crisis) is the most severe form of hypothyroidism. It is rare. A 2025 review in Endocrine Practice describes it as decompensated hypothyroidism with multi-organ failure, low body temperature, and breathing failure. It is usually set off by a trigger such as an infection, cold exposure, or certain medicines.

MedlinePlus lists these signs. Call 911 if someone with known or suspected hypothyroidism has any of them:

  • Below normal body temperature
  • Slow or shallow breathing
  • Low blood pressure or low blood sugar
  • Confusion, extreme sleepiness, or unresponsiveness

Also call 911 for swelling with chest pain or sudden shortness of breath. Contact a doctor right away for one swollen leg that is red or warm, which can mean a clot or infection.

The bottom line

Hypothyroidism can cause puffiness of the face, eyelids, hands, and feet. The swelling comes from water-holding molecules in the skin, so it is often firm and non-pitting. It is a late sign and improves slowly once thyroid levels are normal. Leg swelling alone has many other causes. Pretibial myxedema on the shins is a separate Graves disease problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can hypothyroidism cause puffy eyes in the morning?

Yes. Puffiness around the eyes is a common sign of myxedema in untreated hypothyroidism. Allergies, salt intake, and poor sleep can also cause morning eye puffiness, so a TSH test helps sort it out.

Does levothyroxine reduce water retention?

When hypothyroidism is the cause, bringing thyroid levels back to normal with levothyroxine lets the swelling slowly clear. Improvement usually takes weeks to months. Levothyroxine will not fix swelling caused by heart, kidney, vein, or medicine problems.

Is thyroid swelling pitting or non-pitting?

The classic skin swelling of hypothyroidism is non-pitting, because it comes from glycosaminoglycans in the skin rather than free fluid. Some people with hypothyroidism also have ordinary pitting edema in the ankles from other causes. A doctor can check both.

Can an overactive thyroid cause swollen legs?

Graves disease can rarely cause pretibial myxedema, a lumpy, reddish thickening of the skin on the shins. It is different from fluid swelling and usually appears after thyroid eye disease.

Should I get my thyroid checked for swollen ankles alone?

Ankle swelling on its own is more often caused by veins, heart, kidneys, medicines, or long periods of standing. A TSH test is reasonable if you also have fatigue, cold intolerance, dry skin, or constipation. Ask your doctor to check other causes too.

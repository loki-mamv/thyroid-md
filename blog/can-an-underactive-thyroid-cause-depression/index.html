Can an Underactive Thyroid Cause Depression?

November 28, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Yes, but the link is smaller than many people think. A 2021 meta-analysis in JAMA Psychiatry of 348,014 people found that hypothyroidism raised the odds of clinical depression by about 30%, mostly in overt disease. Treating overt hypothyroidism is standard care, but trials show levothyroxine does little for mood in subclinical hypothyroidism.

Is depression a real symptom of hypothyroidism?

Yes. The NIDDK lists depression among the common symptoms of hypothyroidism, along with fatigue, weight gain, trouble tolerating cold, and a slowed heart rate. The American Thyroid Association says that when thyroid hormone is low, "your body slows down," and you may feel sad or down and forget things easily.

The ATA adds two cautions. Not everyone has all these symptoms, and other health problems can cause them too. A blood test is the only way to know for sure if you have hypothyroidism. The reverse also holds. MedlinePlus notes that some medical conditions, including thyroid disorders, can cause symptoms like depression. That is why low mood and low thyroid are easy to confuse.

So the real question for most readers is not "can it?" but "is it, in my case?" The answer depends on how low your thyroid is, whether it is treated, and how long the low mood has lasted.

How much does low thyroid raise the risk of depression?

Two large meta-analyses give different sizes for the link. The difference comes from how each one defined depression.

  • Depression symptom scores (2018): a meta-analysis in JAMA Psychiatry pooled 19 studies with 36,174 participants. People with autoimmune thyroiditis, Hashimoto's, or subclinical or overt hypothyroidism had 3.56 times the odds of higher scores on depression scales.
  • Clinical depression (2021): the later meta-analysis pooled 25 population studies with 348,014 participants. It looked only at depression that met diagnostic criteria. The odds ratio was 1.30.
  • Overt hypothyroidism: odds ratio 1.77 for clinical depression.
  • Subclinical hypothyroidism: odds ratio 1.13, a small increase.
  • Women vs men: the link held in women (odds ratio 1.48) but not in men (0.71, not significant).

The 2021 authors concluded the link was "considerably lower than previously assumed." In plain terms, low thyroid can tip mood downward, especially when hormone levels are clearly low. But most depression in people with mild thyroid changes is not caused by the thyroid alone.

Does Hashimoto's raise depression risk when thyroid levels are normal?

The evidence is mixed. The 2018 meta-analysis grouped Hashimoto's with hypothyroidism and found a strong link to depression scores. Its studies varied a lot from one another, and the authors found signs that small positive studies were overrepresented for depression.

The 2021 meta-analysis tested thyroid autoimmunity on its own. The odds ratio for clinical depression was 1.24, but its 95% confidence interval included no effect, so the result was inconclusive. The authors wrote that autoimmunity alone may not be the driving factor. If you have positive antibodies and a normal TSH, depression deserves its own evaluation rather than being blamed on the antibodies. Our post on still having symptoms with a normal TSH covers the other causes to check.

Will levothyroxine treatment improve my mood?

It depends on which kind of hypothyroidism you have.

Overt hypothyroidism (high TSH and low free T4) is treated with levothyroxine, a hormone identical to the one a healthy thyroid makes, per the NIDDK. Your doctor checks a blood test about 6 to 8 weeks after you start and adjusts the dose if needed. Because overt disease carries the clearest link to depression, restoring normal levels is the first step. Mood may improve as levels settle, but there is no guarantee.

Subclinical hypothyroidism (high TSH with normal free T4) is different. Trials do not show a mood benefit:

  • In the TRUST trial ancillary study, 427 adults aged 65 or older took levothyroxine or placebo for 12 months. Depression scores did not differ. The adjusted gap was 0.15 points, far below the 2-point change that matters to patients.
  • The same paper updated an earlier meta-analysis and again found no meaningful effect on depressive symptoms.
  • A 2019 BMJ clinical practice guideline reviewed 21 trials with 2,192 participants. Thyroid hormone showed no clinically relevant benefit for quality of life or symptoms, including depressive symptoms and fatigue.

That BMJ panel made a strong recommendation against thyroid hormone for most adults with subclinical hypothyroidism. It does not apply to women trying to become pregnant or to people with a TSH above 20 mIU/L. It may not apply to people with severe symptoms or young adults, such as those 30 or younger. The TRUST data come from older adults, so younger people have less direct evidence. Read more in our guide to subclinical hypothyroidism.

What should you do if you have low thyroid and feel depressed?

This checklist follows the sources above. Use it to plan the conversation with your doctor.

  1. Confirm the thyroid result. The ATA says a blood test is the only way to know. Ask whether your result is overt or subclinical, since the mood evidence differs.
  2. If you are newly treated, give it time. The NIDDK describes a recheck about 6 to 8 weeks after starting levothyroxine. Note how your mood changes as your levels move into range.
  3. If your TSH is already in range, look beyond the thyroid. Depression that persists on a stable, correct dose is unlikely to be fixed by more hormone. Do not raise your dose on your own. Too much thyroid hormone can cause anxiety and a racing heart, as our post on thyroid anxiety and panic attacks explains.
  4. Check the two-week rule. MedlinePlus says depression is diagnosed when symptoms occur most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks. If that fits, ask about depression treatment alongside thyroid care. Our sister site depression.md covers treatment options.
  5. Get help right away in a crisis. The NIMH says anyone having thoughts of suicide can call or text the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline at 988.

Treating both conditions at once is common and safe to discuss. Fixing the thyroid does not rule out depression care, and depression care does not replace thyroid treatment.

The bottom line

An underactive thyroid can contribute to depression, and the NIDDK lists depression as a common symptom. The best population data show a modest link, strongest in overt hypothyroidism and in women. Levothyroxine is the right treatment for overt disease, but trials in subclinical hypothyroidism found no real mood benefit. If low mood lasts on a correct dose, treat it as depression in its own right.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a high TSH make me feel sad even if I feel fine otherwise?

It can, but mild TSH elevations have only a small link to clinical depression. A 2021 meta-analysis found an odds ratio of 1.13 for subclinical hypothyroidism. Low mood with a mildly high TSH often has other causes worth checking.

How long after starting levothyroxine should my mood improve?

No study gives a fixed timeline for mood. The NIDDK says doctors recheck blood levels about 6 to 8 weeks after starting and adjust the dose. If your levels are in range and low mood continues, ask about depression treatment.

Should people with depression get a thyroid test?

MedlinePlus and the NIMH both note that thyroid disorders can cause symptoms like depression. So a doctor may consider a thyroid test when looking into low mood. Ask your doctor whether a TSH test makes sense for you.

Can I take an antidepressant if I have hypothyroidism?

Having hypothyroidism does not by itself mean you cannot be treated for depression. Your doctor will review your medicines together to choose a safe plan.

Is depression more likely with Hashimoto's than with other causes of hypothyroidism?

Not clearly. A 2018 meta-analysis found higher depression scores in autoimmune thyroiditis, but a larger 2021 analysis found the link with autoimmunity alone was inconclusive. In the 2021 analysis, overt hypothyroidism showed the clearest link.
